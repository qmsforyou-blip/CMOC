### SOURCE

### LOCATION
Clause 7.5 — Documented information; 7.5.1; 7.5.2; 7.5.3; Clause 3.10; Annex A.2(e); Annex A.7.5

### SOURCE CLAIM

The quality management system shall include: documented information required by the document; and documented information determined by the organization as being necessary for the effectiveness of the quality management system. The extent can differ according to organization size and activities, complexity of processes and interactions, and competence of persons. fileciteturn74file2

When creating and updating documented information, the organization shall ensure appropriate identification and description, format and media, and review and approval for suitability and adequacy. fileciteturn74file7

Documented information required by the QMS and by the document shall be controlled to ensure availability and suitability for use where and when needed, and adequate protection from loss of confidentiality, improper use or loss of integrity. Applicable control activities include distribution, access, retrieval and use; storage and preservation including legibility; control of changes such as version control; and retention and disposition. Necessary documented information of external origin shall be identified as appropriate and controlled. Documented information available as evidence of conformity shall be protected from unintended alterations. Access can imply permission to view only, or permission and authority to view and change. fileciteturn74file11

Clause 3.10 defines documented information as information required to be controlled and maintained by the organization and the medium on which it is contained. It can be in any format, media and from any source, and can refer to the management system and related processes, information created for operation (documentation), or evidence of results achieved (records). fileciteturn73file12

Annex A.2(e) explains a terminology change aligned with the Harmonized Structure: “shall be available as documented information” replaces “maintain documented information” for documentation, while “documented information shall be available as evidence of” replaces “retain documented information as evidence of” for records. “Available” means that the organization can obtain, use or provide the information. “As evidence of” indicates objective evidence to be retained and is not a requirement to meet legal evidential requirements. fileciteturn75file17

Annex A.7.5 states that the organization determines documented information needed to ensure and demonstrate conformity, beyond information required by the document or customers/other relevant interested parties. The decision to document information can result from process-control needs or the need to provide evidence of conformity to third parties. Documented information can codify procedures, avoid deviations, capitalize knowledge, maintain history of operations, facilitate communications and protect organizational knowledge. Effectiveness of the QMS does not necessarily depend on the amount of documented information produced. Information can be in any medium and managed by any means; emerging technologies facilitate availability but introduce risks such as massive information loss and loss of confidentiality. Distribution, access and obsolete-information controls are important, and evidence of conformity must be protected from unintended alteration. fileciteturn75file0

### DISTINCTIONS

**DIS-001 — DOCUMENTED INFORMATION ≠ DOCUMENTATION**

Clause 3.10 uses documented information as the broader term and says it can refer to information created for the organization to operate (documentation).

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-002 — DOCUMENTED INFORMATION ≠ RECORDS**

The same definition states that documented information can refer to evidence of results achieved (records). Therefore “documented information” is broader than the referent “records”.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-003 — DOCUMENTATION ≠ RECORDS**

The Source distinguishes information created for operation (documentation) from evidence of results achieved (records).

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-004 — DOCUMENTED INFORMATION ≠ INFORMATION ONLY**

The definition includes both information and the medium on which it is contained.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-005 — REQUIRED DOCUMENTED INFORMATION ≠ ALL POSSIBLE DOCUMENTED INFORMATION**

The QMS includes information required by the document plus information the organization determines necessary for QMS effectiveness. Annex A explicitly states that effectiveness does not necessarily depend on the amount produced.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-006 — AVAILABILITY ≠ CONTROL**

Availability and suitability for use are outcomes to ensure; control includes a broader set of activities such as distribution, access, retrieval, storage, change control, retention and disposition.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-007 — ACCESS TO VIEW ≠ AUTHORITY TO CHANGE**

The Note to 7.5.3.2 explicitly distinguishes permission to view from permission and authority to view and change.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-008 — CURRENT USE ≠ OBSOLETE USE**

Obsolete documented information, if retained, requires suitable identification or controls to prevent unintended use.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-009 — INFORMATION ≠ MEDIUM**

Documented information comprises information and the medium on which it is contained.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-010 — DOCUMENTED INFORMATION ≠ FIXED FORMAT / MEDIA**

The Source allows any format and media, including paper and electronic, and examples such as language, software version and graphics.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

**DIS-011 — CREATION ≠ UPDATE**

7.5.2 treats creating and updating documented information as controlled activities requiring identification/description, format/media and review/approval.

**STATUS: NEW / SINGLE-SOURCE**

### DOCUMENT / DOCUMENTED INFORMATION / RECORD

This is the central result of the wagonette.

The Source's terminology is hierarchical in function:

```text
DOCUMENTED INFORMATION
        ├── DOCUMENTATION
        └── RECORDS
```

But we must be precise: Clause 3.10 says documented information **can refer to** these categories; it does not establish them as independent ontological classes in CMOC.

Therefore:

```text
DOCUMENTED INFORMATION
        ≠
DOCUMENTATION
        ≠
RECORD
```

is a useful CMOC distinction supported by the Source, while the exact CMOC ontological relationship remains **CANDIDATE**.

The Annex also makes an important terminology transition: the current wording uses “available as documented information” rather than the older “maintain documented information” for documentation, and “documented information shall be available as evidence of” rather than “retain documented information as evidence of” for records. fileciteturn75file17

### CMOC INTERPRETATION

This wagonette changes the status of our earlier discussion about `Document → Documented information → Record`.

The strongest Source-grounded structure is not a simple linear chain but an umbrella:

```text
                 DOCUMENTED INFORMATION
                         │
             ┌───────────┴───────────┐
             ↓                       ↓
      DOCUMENTATION                RECORDS
      information created       evidence of results
      for operation             achieved
```

This is a **CMOC interpretation of Clause 3.10**, not a finalized Core ontology.

The second major structure is lifecycle control:

```text
NEED
 ↓
CREATE / UPDATE
 ↓
IDENTIFY
 ↓
REVIEW / APPROVE
 ↓
USE / ACCESS
 ↓
PRESERVE
 ↓
CHANGE CONTROL
 ↓
RETAIN / DISPOSE
```

The third is especially important for CMOC:

> **Documented information is not valuable merely because it exists; it is valuable when it is available, suitable for use, controlled and protected for its intended function.**

This is CMOC interpretation.

And one further architectural point:

> **The Source does not equate documentation with organizational effectiveness. It explicitly rejects the assumption that more documented information necessarily means a more effective QMS.**

### CROSS-CLAUSE OBSERVATION

ISO-024 established that organizational knowledge can be undocumented.

ISO-025 established that documented information can serve as evidence of competence without being competence itself.

ISO-026 did not create a general documentation requirement for awareness.

ISO-027 treated communication as a process without making documentation a universal requirement.

ISO-028 now provides the explicit architecture for documented information and confirms that it is a controlled medium for both operational information and evidence of results.

A stronger multi-source candidate therefore emerges:

```text
KNOWLEDGE
   ≠
DOCUMENTED INFORMATION
   ≠
COMPETENCE

DOCUMENTED INFORMATION
   ├── DOCUMENTATION
   └── RECORD / EVIDENCE OF RESULT
```

And another:

```text
RESULT / CONFORMITY
        ↓
EVIDENCE
        ↓
DOCUMENTED INFORMATION
        ↓
PROTECTION / AVAILABILITY / CONTROL
```

The second structure connects directly with ISO-023's result-validity line, but remains a **MULTI-CONFIRMATION CANDIDATE** until later clauses provide further evidence.
