# GM-096 — Machine Candidate Passport: Supplier Capability Assessment

**Source:** GM Quality Systems Basics rev. March 2009
**Provenance:** GM SOURCE → SOURCE-FINAL → Distinctions → Mechanisms → Machine Candidates → Catalog Cross-Check
**Status:** MACHINE-CANDIDATE / NON-CANON
**Candidate:** Supplier Capability Assessment

## 1. Identity

**Candidate type:** external-capability assessment / development machine candidate

**Primary source realization:** Supply Chain Management / Supplier Assessment

## 2. Problem

A supplier's ability to satisfy requirements cannot be established reliably from purchase status or a single delivered result. GM uses structured supplier assessments, performance measures and development activities to evaluate and improve supplier capability.

## 3. Input

A candidate or existing supplier together with applicable customer, quality-system, product, process and/or special-process requirements.

## 4. Trigger

Supplier qualification, periodic assessment, identified performance gap, special-process requirement, or another defined need to establish supplier capability.

## 5. Operating sequence

`Requirements → Assessment → Evidence/Rating → Gap → Development Action → Reassessment`

Depending on the assessment context, the source uses activities such as Potential Supplier Assessment, Quality Systems Basics Audit, labeling audit, special-process audits and Drill Deep & Wide Audit.

## 6. Output

An observable assessment of supplier capability, identified gaps and, where required, development actions and a route to reassessment.

## 7. State transition

`UNKNOWN / UNASSESSED CAPABILITY → ASSESSED CAPABILITY / STATUS`

and, where a gap exists:

`CAPABILITY GAP → DEVELOPMENT ACTION → REASSESSED CAPABILITY`

The machine therefore does not merely classify a supplier; it can initiate a controlled development loop.

## 8. Owner / responsibility

The source places supplier-management responsibilities across GM purchasing, quality and supplier-development functions, with supplier participation in corrective/development activity. Exact ownership varies by assessment type and is not collapsed into one universal role here.

## 9. Evidence

Source-derived evidence includes:

- supplier assessment/audit results;
- quality-system assessment;
- special-process assessment;
- supplier performance metrics;
- ratings/status;
- corrective/development actions;
- reassessment results.

## 10. Acceptance condition

The assessment cycle is complete when the applicable assessment has been performed, its result/status is recorded, required gaps/actions are identified, and the applicable decision or development path is established.

For development cases, completion of an action is not itself treated as proof of capability; reassessment is the relevant confirmation step.

## 11. Failure / gap conditions

A supplier may be placed into an improvement/development path when requirements are not met or capability gaps are identified. The source also distinguishes performance status from the underlying capability that the status represents.

No universal numerical threshold is inferred here because the source uses different assessment and performance criteria in different contexts.

## 12. Distinctions implemented

- Compliance ≠ Capability
- Supplier ≠ Purchase Source
- Assessment ≠ Development
- Performance Status ≠ Capability itself
- Corrective Action ≠ Demonstrated Capability
- Requirement ≠ Evidence of Capability

## 13. Boundary

This machine does **not**:

- replace supplier selection or commercial purchasing decisions;
- equate a good single delivery with supplier capability;
- treat an assessment score as the capability itself;
- close a development issue merely because an action was assigned;
- replace product/process-specific validation or approval.

Its boundary is:

> **Assess and develop the capability of an external supplier to meet defined requirements, using evidence, status, action and reassessment.**

## 14. Relationship to other candidate Machines

### Supplier Capability Assessment → Supplier Performance Statusing

Assessment generates evidence that can feed performance status and ratings. Statusing may be a supporting mechanism rather than a separate Machine.

### Supplier Capability Assessment ↔ Systemic Problem Resolution

Supplier problems may trigger systemic problem resolution. Conversely, recurring systemic failures can become evidence of a supplier capability gap.

### Supplier Capability Assessment ↔ Requirement Operationalization

Supplier requirements must be communicated and made operational before capability can be meaningfully assessed.

### Supplier Capability Assessment ↔ Nonconforming Product Control

A nonconforming delivery is an event/evidence source; it is not by itself equivalent to an assessment of supplier capability.

## 15. Reusability hypothesis

The candidate may be reusable beyond supplier management wherever an organization depends on an external actor whose capability to satisfy requirements must be assessed, developed and reassessed.

This broader reuse is a CMOC hypothesis and requires confirmation from additional sources.

## 17. Architectural observation

The important CMOC contribution is the separation:

`Supplier → Performance → Assessment → Capability Judgment → Development → Reassessment`

rather than:

`Supplier → Delivery → Pass/Fail`

The source therefore treats an external organization as a potentially managed capability, not merely as a transaction source.
