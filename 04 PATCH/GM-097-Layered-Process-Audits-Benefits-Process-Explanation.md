# GM-097 — Layered Process Audits — Benefits & Process Explanation

## SOURCE

**Source:** GM Quality System Basics Overview Supplier Audit.pdf  
**Section:** 7.1 Benefits; 7.2 Process explanation  
**Pages:** 226–227  
**Status:** SOURCE-SUPPORTED extraction; CMOC interpretations explicitly separated.

---

## 1. SOURCE EXTRACTION

### p. 226 — 7.1 Benefits

SOURCE states that Layered Process Audits provide a system to:

- verify compliance to the documented process;
- instill discipline;
- improve communication;
- improve overall quality.

SOURCE further states that LPA ensures a high level of process control by identifying and controlling **high risk / significant process elements**.

SOURCE states that LPA maintains proper application of standards as defined and achieved through the **operational readiness process**.

SOURCE states that LPA identifies opportunities for improvement and provides a process for effective follow-up.

### p. 227 — 7.2 Process explanation

SOURCE defines a Layered Process Audit (LPA) as a standardized audit performed on a regular, frequent basis by **all layers of the organization** to verify adherence to operational standards.

SOURCE states that LPAs are an industry standard.

SOURCE states that LPAs supplement ongoing control plan and job instruction checks.

SOURCE states that LPAs shall be owned by manufacturing leadership, identified as **Team Leader – Plant / Operations Manager**.

SOURCE states that Quality and other functions participate and support the LPA system.

## 8. CMOC INTERPRETATION

Pages 226–227 substantially strengthen the definition of LPA compared with the introductory page 225.

The source now establishes that LPA is not simply an audit performed by management. It is a **standardized, regular and frequent audit performed by all layers of the organization**, owned by manufacturing leadership and supported by Quality and other functions.

The benefit statement also gives LPA a broader role than simple compliance verification: discipline, communication, overall quality, high-risk process control, standards maintenance and improvement follow-up are all explicitly named.

The strongest SOURCE-supported structural reading at this stage is:

```text
STANDARD
   ↓
LAYERED / FREQUENT VERIFICATION
   ↓
PROCESS CONTROL
   ↓
IMPROVEMENT OPPORTUNITY
   ↓
FOLLOW-UP
```

The detailed operational implementation is deliberately left open for the next source pages.
