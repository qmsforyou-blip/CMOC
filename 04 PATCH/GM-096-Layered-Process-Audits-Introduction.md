## 2. DISTINCTIONS

### D1 — LPA is introduced as a layered process-control mechanism

**Status: SOURCE-SUPPORTED**

The source establishes a dedicated section for **Layered Process Audits** and assigns it a defined purpose, scope, responsibility and subsequent process architecture.

### D2 — LPA purpose includes consistent execution of standards

**Status: SOURCE-SUPPORTED**

The first stated purpose is to ensure consistent application and execution of standards.

### D3 — LPA combines quality control with leadership/operator coaching

**Status: SOURCE-SUPPORTED**

The second purpose connects built-in quality and increased operator/leadership awareness with coaching/teaching interaction between leadership and operators.

This is important: SOURCE does not present LPA solely as an inspection activity; the stated purpose explicitly includes an interaction/learning component.

### D4 — LPA scope extends beyond manufacturing operations

**Status: SOURCE-SUPPORTED**

The listed scope includes assembly, manufacturing, shipping/receiving, all operations and other support functions.

### D5 — Ownership is assigned to Plant / Operations Manager

**Status: SOURCE-SUPPORTED**

SOURCE assigns ownership to the Plant / Operations Mgr.

### D6 — Contingency planning is part of the introductory responsibility block

**Status: SOURCE-SUPPORTED**

The introduction explicitly lists **Contingency Plan for All Situations** under Responsibility.

### D7 — LPA process is decomposed into a sequence of control elements

**Status: SOURCE-SUPPORTED**

The outline establishes a process structure running from schedule/tracking and high-risk item development through checklist concept/evaluation, countermeasures and management review, followed by a “Summary, Shalls” section.

The detailed mechanics are not yet available on pp. 224–225 and must be extracted from the subsequent pages.

### D8 — High-risk items are an explicit input to the LPA process

**Status: SOURCE-SUPPORTED**

The outline explicitly includes **Develop high risk items for auditing** as §7.2.2. This establishes high-risk items as a named component of the LPA method, but does not yet define how risk is determined.

## 8. CMOC INTERPRETATION

Pages 224–225 materially change the status of LPA compared with p. 223.

Page 223 merely announced the topic. Pages 224–225 establish that LPA is a **defined management mechanism** with:

1. an explicit purpose;
2. broad organizational scope;
3. an identified owner;
4. a process decomposition;
5. explicit high-risk audit items;
6. a checklist mechanism;
7. countermeasures; and
8. management review.

The most significant SOURCE distinction is the dual purpose:

> **consistent execution of standards + built-in quality/awareness through leadership–operator coaching/teaching interaction.**

This prevents reducing LPA to a conventional quality audit. However, the precise meaning of “layered” and the mechanics of the layers are not yet defined on these pages.

The phrase **“All Operations”** in the scope should also not be collapsed into “manufacturing”: SOURCE separately lists Assembly, Manufacturing Operations, Shipping/Receiving, All Operations and Other Support Functions.
