# GM-018 — Lessons Learned

**Извещение на изменение:** 0129+280826  
**SOURCE:** GM Quality System Basics rev. March 2009  
**Location:** FAST RESPONSE → 1.4 Lessons Learned, pp. 47–49  
**Status:** CANDIDATE / SINGLE-SOURCE

## SOURCE

GM defines a Lessons Learned system as a process for capturing information that supports continual improvement to operations/processes, prevents repeated mistakes, and allows the organization to capitalize on successes. It applies to all functions and responsibilities, so everyone should participate. Documentation supporting continuous improvement is to be entered into the Lessons Learned system, including examples such as Master PFMEA, Problem Solving, and Read Across.

GM further states that Lessons Learned may be identified by anyone. A disciplined approach to problem prevention using Lessons Learned shall be established. Examples include APQP, LPA, Error Proofing Verification failures, Problem Solving, Verification Station findings, Continuous Improvement Teams, Risk Reduction/Reverse PFMEA, suggestion programs, and business/quality operating system management reviews. GM Drill Wide Read Across communication and follow-up and APQP reviews of Lessons Learned are also identified.

Lessons Learned shall be documented. Examples of documentation include a Lessons Learned Form, APQP Checklist, Master PFMEA, computer form or website. Lessons Learned shall be communicated and kept available to current and potential users. Leadership shall review the Lessons Learned process to assure implementation.

## TERMS

Lessons Learned; Lessons Learned system; continual improvement; repeated mistakes; successes; problem prevention; Read Across; documentation; communication; availability; implementation; leadership review.

## DISTINCTIONS

### DIS-GM-018-01
**Problem Solving result ≠ Lessons Learned.**

A problem-solving result can become input to a Lessons Learned system, but the system exists for broader organizational learning and prevention.

### DIS-GM-018-02
**Lessons Learned ≠ individual knowledge.**

GM requires a system for capturing, documenting, communicating and making Lessons Learned available.

### DIS-GM-018-03
**Capture ≠ institutionalization.**

Information must be documented, communicated, made available, and its implementation reviewed by leadership.

### DIS-GM-018-04
**Lessons Learned ≠ only quality-department activity.**

GM explicitly applies it to all functions and responsibilities and states that anyone may identify a Lesson Learned.

### DIS-GM-018-05
**Lessons Learned ≠ retrospective archive only.**

GM explicitly connects Lessons Learned to problem prevention and to future operations/processes.

## MECHANISM

### CANDIDATE — Organizational Learning Transfer Mechanism

**Input:** information/knowledge generated by quality, problem-solving, improvement and other organizational activities.

**Transformation:** capture → document → communicate/make available → apply/read across → review implementation.

**Output:** organizationally available Lesson Learned that can support future prevention and improvement.

**Organizational effect:** learning is transferred from an individual event or local activity into a reusable organizational resource.

## CMOC INTERPRETATION

The strongest CMOC-level extraction is not “Lessons Learned = knowledge.” It is:

> **Experience becomes organizational knowledge only when the organization has a mechanism that captures it, makes it retrievable/available, transfers it to potential users, and checks implementation.**

This is an interpretation of the GM construction, not a GM definition of CMOC.

The particularly important architectural transition is:

```text
LOCAL EXPERIENCE
      ↓
DOCUMENTED LESSON
      ↓
ORGANIZATIONAL AVAILABILITY
      ↓
READ ACROSS / APPLICATION
      ↓
FUTURE PREVENTION
```

This gives a candidate connection from the Fast Response / Problem Solving assembly into organizational memory and prevention, while remaining distinct from the individual Problem Solving process.
